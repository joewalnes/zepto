ID:       QA-NAV-019
NAME:     Sticky/goal column survives shorter lines
PRIORITY: P1
FEATURE:  arrow pageupdown sticky-column
RELATED:  QA-NAV-008, QA-NAV-009, QA-NAV-018

Discoverability: pure cursor-movement behavior, not a command — nothing to
find in the palette because there is nothing to turn on. It should just
feel like the expected, unsurprising way arrow keys/Page Up/Page Down
already behave (this is the standard "sticky column" pattern most editors
implement). Reported live by the user 2026-09-01: "As I move cursor down
across lines (either via mouse drag or arrows), the column indicator on
top ruler jumps around due to lines ending before cursor position."

SETUP
  File with a long line, two short lines, then a long line again, e.g.:
    This is a somewhat long line for column te
    short
    hi
    This is another somewhat long line for testing

STEPS
  1. Move cursor to col 30 on line 1.
  2. Press Down twice (through the two short lines).
  3. Press Down once more (onto the second long line).
  4. Press Up three times (back to line 1).
  5. From a short line, press Left, then Down to the next short line,
     then Up twice back to line 1.
  6. From col 30 on line 1, Down onto a short line, then type a character.

VERIFY
  - Step 2: cursor is pinned at the TRUE end of each short line (col 6 on
    "short", col 3 on "hi") — never drawn past the actual text.
  - Step 3: cursor lands back at col 30 - the original goal column is
    restored exactly, not left at wherever the short line's end was.
  - Step 4: same restoration holds on the way back up.
  - Step 5: Left-arrow (explicit horizontal movement) resets the goal to
    the new real column - the OLD goal (30) is not what gets restored.
  - Step 6: typed character lands immediately after the short line's true
    end (no whitespace padding out toward the old goal column); saved
    file content confirms no stray spaces were inserted. New goal becomes
    the post-type column.
  - Page Up/Down (QA-NAV-008/009/018) go through the identical View.pm
    code path and are covered there — this test focuses on plain arrows,
    Home/End reset, and the type-past-end interaction that those don't.
